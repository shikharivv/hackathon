## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the performance review framework at Meridian Technologies, Inc. ("the Company"). Regular and fair performance evaluations align individual contributions with organizational goals, support professional development, and inform compensation and career progression decisions.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all regular full-time and part-time employees who have completed their 90-day probation period (see POL-004 Onboarding Guide, Section 7). Probationary employees are evaluated through the separate probation review process.

## 3. Review Cycle

### 3.1 Semi-Annual Reviews

3.1.1. Formal performance reviews are conducted **twice per year**:
- **Mid-Year Review:** Conducted in **June** (review period: January 1 – May 31)
- **Year-End Review:** Conducted in **December** (review period: June 1 – November 30)

3.1.2. The review timeline is as follows:

| Phase | Mid-Year | Year-End |
|-------|----------|----------|
| Self-assessment window opens | June 1 | December 1 |
| Self-assessment deadline | June 10 | December 10 |
| 360-degree feedback collection | June 1 – June 15 | December 1 – December 15 |
| Manager review drafting | June 11 – June 20 | December 11 – December 20 |
| Calibration sessions | June 21 – June 25 | December 21 – December 24 |
| Review meetings with employees | June 26 – June 30 | January 2 – January 10 |

### 3.2 Continuous Feedback

3.2.1. In addition to formal semi-annual reviews, managers are expected to provide ongoing feedback through:
- **Bi-weekly 1:1 meetings** (minimum frequency; weekly is encouraged)
- Informal feedback in real-time following notable contributions or areas for improvement
- Quarterly goal progress check-ins

3.2.2. Continuous feedback is documented in the HRIS and may be referenced during formal reviews.

## 4. Rating Scale

### 4.1 Five-Point Scale

All employees are evaluated using the following rating scale:

| Rating | Descriptor | Definition |
|--------|------------|------------|
| 5 | **Exceptional** | Consistently exceeds all expectations; demonstrates outstanding impact beyond role scope; serves as a role model for the organization |
| 4 | **Exceeds Expectations** | Regularly surpasses expectations in most areas; delivers high-quality results with minimal guidance; takes on additional responsibilities |
| 3 | **Meets Expectations** | Consistently meets the requirements of the role; delivers reliable, quality work; meets established goals |
| 2 | **Below Expectations** | Partially meets expectations; performance gaps identified in one or more key areas; requires improvement with support |
| 1 | **Unsatisfactory** | Fails to meet minimum expectations; significant performance deficiencies; immediate improvement required |

### 4.2 Rating Guidelines

4.2.1. A rating of **3 (Meets Expectations)** represents solid, dependable performance and is the expected standard. Most employees should fall within the 3–4 range.

4.2.2. A rating of **5 (Exceptional)** should be reserved for truly outstanding performance that demonstrably advances team or Company objectives. This rating is expected for no more than **10–15%** of employees in any given review cycle.

4.2.3. A rating of **2 (Below Expectations)** triggers a Performance Improvement Plan (see Section 8). A rating of **1 (Unsatisfactory)** triggers an accelerated PIP or immediate management action.

4.2.4. Ratings are assigned across multiple competency areas:
- **Core Job Performance:** Quality, timeliness, and effectiveness of work output
- **Collaboration and Communication:** Teamwork, stakeholder engagement, knowledge sharing
- **Innovation and Problem-Solving:** Creative thinking, initiative, process improvement
- **Leadership (managers only):** People management, team development, strategic thinking
- **Values Alignment:** Adherence to Company values and POL-006 Code of Conduct

## 5. Self-Assessment

### 5.1 Requirements

5.1.1. All employees must complete a self-assessment as part of each review cycle.

5.1.2. The self-assessment should include:
- Summary of key accomplishments during the review period
- Progress against established goals and OKRs (Section 10)
- Areas of growth and development
- Challenges faced and how they were addressed
- Goals and development priorities for the next period

5.1.3. Self-assessments must be submitted through the HRIS by the published deadline (see Section 3.1.2).

### 5.2 Manager Review of Self-Assessment

5.2.1. Managers must review the self-assessment before drafting their evaluation.

5.2.2. The self-assessment serves as a discussion starting point and does not predetermine the manager's rating.

## 6. 360-Degree Feedback

### 6.1 Process

6.1.1. As part of each review cycle, employees receive anonymous feedback from **3–5 peers** selected in collaboration between the employee and their manager.

6.1.2. Peer reviewers may include direct reports (for managers), cross-functional collaborators, and internal stakeholders.

6.1.3. Feedback is collected through a structured questionnaire covering collaboration, communication, reliability, and professional behavior.

### 6.2 Use of Feedback

6.2.1. 360-degree feedback is used to supplement the manager's evaluation and identify blind spots.

6.2.2. Feedback is presented in aggregate form to protect anonymity. Individual responses are never attributed to specific reviewers.

6.2.3. Employees receive a summary of their 360-degree feedback during the review meeting.

## 7. Calibration Sessions

### 7.1 Purpose

7.1.1. Calibration sessions are conducted among managers within each department (or division) to ensure consistency and fairness in ratings across teams.

7.1.2. During calibration, managers present their proposed ratings and rationale for each direct report. Peers and the department head may challenge or support the proposed ratings.

### 7.2 Calibration Guidelines

7.2.1. Expected rating distribution guidelines (not quotas):
- Rating 5: 10–15%
- Rating 4: 25–35%
- Rating 3: 40–50%
- Rating 2: 5–10%
- Rating 1: 0–5%

7.2.2. Deviations from expected distributions must be justified with documented evidence.

7.2.3. Human Resources facilitates calibration sessions and resolves disputes.

7.2.4. Ratings may be adjusted as a result of calibration. Managers must communicate any changes to the employee with clear rationale.

## 8. Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

### 8.1 Initiation

8.1.1. A PIP is initiated when an employee receives a rating of **2 (Below Expectations)** or lower in a formal review, or when a manager identifies sustained performance issues outside the review cycle.

8.1.2. A PIP may also be initiated as part of the disciplinary process outlined in POL-006 Code of Conduct, Section 10.

### 8.2 PIP Structure

8.2.1. Each PIP must include the following elements:
- **Specific performance gaps:** Clear description of the areas requiring improvement
- **Expected standards:** Measurable criteria the employee must meet
- **Support and resources:** Training, coaching, or mentoring provided to assist the employee
- **Timeline:** A defined improvement period of **30, 60, or 90 days** depending on the severity and nature of the performance issues
- **Check-in schedule:** Regular meetings (minimum weekly) between the employee and manager to review progress
- **Consequences:** Clear statement that failure to meet the improvement criteria may result in further disciplinary action, including termination

### 8.3 PIP Outcomes

8.3.1. At the conclusion of the PIP period, the manager will assess the employee's progress and determine one of the following outcomes:
- **Successful completion:** The employee has met the required standards. The PIP is closed, and the employee returns to normal performance management.
- **Extension:** Some progress has been made, but additional time is needed. The PIP may be extended once for up to **30 additional days**.
- **Termination:** The employee has not met the required standards despite support. The manager may proceed with termination in accordance with Company policy, after consultation with Human Resources and Legal.

### 8.4 Documentation

8.4.1. All PIP documents, progress notes, and outcomes must be recorded in the HRIS and retained for a minimum of **5 years**.

8.4.2. The employee receives a copy of all PIP documentation and has the right to submit a written response.

## 9. Connection to Compensation

### 9.1 Merit Increases

9.1.1. The year-end review rating directly influences annual merit increases, effective **April 1** of the following year.

9.1.2. Merit increase guidelines by rating:

| Rating | Typical Merit Increase Range |
|--------|------------------------------|
| 5 — Exceptional | 6–10% |
| 4 — Exceeds Expectations | 3–6% |
| 3 — Meets Expectations | 2–3% |
| 2 — Below Expectations | 0% |
| 1 — Unsatisfactory | 0% |

9.1.3. Actual merit increases are subject to the Company's annual compensation budget and market data analysis.

### 9.2 Bonus Eligibility

9.2.1. Employees with a year-end rating of **3 or higher** are eligible for the annual performance bonus, paid in **Q1** of the following year.

9.2.2. Employees on a PIP at year-end are not eligible for a performance bonus.

### 9.3 Promotion Consideration

9.3.1. Employees must have a rating of **4 or higher** for two consecutive review cycles to be considered for promotion.

9.3.2. Promotion decisions also consider role availability, organizational need, and leadership readiness (for managerial promotions).

## 10. Goal-Setting Framework (OKRs)

### 10.1 Structure

10.1.1. The Company uses **Objectives and Key Results (OKRs)** as the primary goal-setting framework.

10.1.2. Each employee should have **3–5 Objectives** per review period, each with **2–4 measurable Key Results**.

10.1.3. OKRs should be:
- Aligned with team and Company-level objectives
- Specific, measurable, and time-bound
- A stretch beyond baseline expectations (achieving 70% of OKRs is considered successful)

### 10.2 OKR Setting Process

10.2.1. Employees draft their OKRs in collaboration with their manager at the beginning of each review period (January and July).

10.2.2. OKRs are finalized and documented in the HRIS within the **first 2 weeks** of the review period.

10.2.3. OKRs may be adjusted mid-cycle with manager approval if business priorities change.

### 10.3 OKR Assessment

10.3.1. OKR completion rates are a key input to the performance rating but are not the sole determinant.

10.3.2. Managers should consider the difficulty of objectives, external factors affecting outcomes, and the quality of effort when assessing OKR results.

## 11. Documentation and Recordkeeping

11.1. All performance review records, including self-assessments, manager evaluations, 360-degree feedback summaries, calibration outcomes, and PIP documentation, are stored securely in the HRIS.

11.2. Records are retained for a minimum of **7 years** following the employee's separation from the Company.

11.3. Employees may request access to their own performance records at any time through the HRIS self-service portal.

## 12. Appeal Process

### 12.1 Grounds for Appeal

12.1.1. Employees may appeal their performance rating if they believe:
- The rating was based on inaccurate or incomplete information
- The process was not followed as outlined in this policy
- Bias or discrimination influenced the rating

### 12.2 Appeal Procedure

12.2.1. Appeals must be submitted in writing to Human Resources within **10 business days** of receiving the performance review.

12.2.2. The appeal must include specific reasons and supporting evidence.

12.2.3. Human Resources will convene a review panel consisting of the HR Business Partner, the skip-level manager, and one additional manager from outside the employee's department.

12.2.4. The review panel will issue a decision within **15 business days** of receiving the appeal. The panel's decision is final.

12.2.5. The appeal process is conducted confidentially. Employees are protected from retaliation for filing an appeal in good faith.

## 13. Manager Responsibilities

13.1. Managers are responsible for:
- Providing timely and constructive feedback throughout the review period
- Completing evaluations accurately and by the published deadlines
- Participating fully in calibration sessions
- Conducting review meetings in a private, respectful setting
- Documenting all performance-related discussions
- Supporting employees on PIPs with appropriate resources and check-ins

13.2. Managers who consistently fail to meet their performance management responsibilities may have this reflected in their own performance review.

## 14. Policy Administration

14.1. The Talent Management team within Human Resources is responsible for the administration and interpretation of this policy.

14.2. Questions should be directed to talentmgmt@meridiantech.com.
