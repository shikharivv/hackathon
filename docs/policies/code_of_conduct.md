## 1. Purpose

This Code of Conduct establishes the ethical and behavioral standards expected of all individuals working at or on behalf of Meridian Technologies, Inc. ("the Company"). It provides a framework for decision-making that reflects the Company's values and commitment to integrity, respect, and accountability.

## 2. Scope

This Code applies to all employees (full-time, part-time, temporary), officers, directors, contractors, consultants, and any third party acting on the Company's behalf. All references to "employees" in this document encompass these groups unless otherwise specified.

## 3. Company Values and Principles

The Company is founded on the following core values:

3.1. **Integrity:** We act honestly and transparently in all interactions. We do what is right, even when it is difficult.

3.2. **Respect:** We treat all individuals with dignity, fairness, and empathy. We value diverse perspectives and foster an inclusive environment.

3.3. **Excellence:** We hold ourselves to high standards in the quality of our work. We pursue continuous improvement and innovation.

3.4. **Accountability:** We take ownership of our actions and their outcomes. We honor our commitments to colleagues, customers, and stakeholders.

3.5. **Collaboration:** We work together across teams and functions to achieve shared goals. We communicate openly and support one another.

## 4. Professional Behavior Standards

### 4.1 General Expectations

4.1.1. Employees are expected to conduct themselves in a professional manner at all times, whether in the office, working remotely, or representing the Company at external events.

4.1.2. Professional behavior includes punctuality, preparedness, respectful communication, appropriate attire (business casual or role-appropriate), and constructive engagement with colleagues and stakeholders.

4.1.3. Employees must comply with all applicable laws, regulations, and Company policies.

### 4.2 Workplace Communication

4.2.1. All workplace communications, including email, instant messaging, and video calls, should be professional, clear, and respectful.

4.2.2. Employees should avoid sarcasm, passive-aggressive language, or any communication that could be perceived as hostile or demeaning.

4.2.3. Meetings should start and end on time. Participants should be prepared and engaged. See POL-003 Remote Work Policy, Section 7 for meeting expectations in hybrid environments.

## 5. Anti-Harassment and Discrimination

### 5.1 Zero Tolerance Policy

5.1.1. The Company maintains a **zero-tolerance policy** for harassment, discrimination, and retaliation based on any protected characteristic, including but not limited to race, color, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, or genetic information.

5.1.2. This policy applies to all work-related settings, including the office, remote work environments, Company-sponsored events, business travel, and online communications.

### 5.2 Definitions

5.2.1. **Harassment** includes any unwelcome conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment. Examples include:
- Offensive jokes, slurs, or name-calling
- Physical intimidation, threats, or assault
- Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature
- Display of offensive images or materials
- Cyberbullying or online harassment

5.2.2. **Discrimination** includes making employment decisions (hiring, firing, promotion, compensation, assignments) based on protected characteristics rather than merit and qualifications.

### 5.3 Reporting

5.3.1. Employees who experience or witness harassment or discrimination are encouraged to report the behavior immediately. Reports may be made to:
- The employee's direct manager
- Human Resources (hr@meridiantech.com)
- The Ethics Hotline (1-800-555-0188, available 24/7, anonymous reporting available)

5.3.2. See Section 8 for full reporting procedures.

## 6. Conflict of Interest

### 6.1 Definition

6.1.1. A conflict of interest arises when an employee's personal interests, relationships, or activities interfere or appear to interfere with their ability to act in the Company's best interest.

### 6.2 Common Examples

6.2.1. Financial interests in a competitor, supplier, or customer of the Company.

6.2.2. Outside employment or consulting that conflicts with Company duties or uses Company resources.

6.2.3. Hiring, supervising, or making business decisions involving a family member or close personal relationship.

6.2.4. Using Company information, relationships, or resources for personal gain.

### 6.3 Disclosure and Management

6.3.1. Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest to their manager and the Ethics & Compliance team **promptly** upon becoming aware of the conflict.

6.3.2. The Ethics & Compliance team will assess the conflict and determine appropriate mitigation measures, which may include recusal from decision-making, divestiture, or restructuring of reporting relationships.

6.3.3. Employees must complete an annual **Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form** as part of the Company's annual compliance certification.

## 7. Confidentiality and Data Protection

### 7.1 Confidential Information

7.1.1. Employees have access to confidential and proprietary information as part of their roles. This includes trade secrets, business strategies, financial data, customer information, employee records, and intellectual property.

7.1.2. Confidential information must not be disclosed to any unauthorized party, inside or outside the Company, without proper authorization.

7.1.3. The obligation to protect confidential information continues after separation from the Company, as outlined in the employee's confidentiality agreement.

### 7.2 Data Protection

7.2.1. Employees must handle personal data (employee, customer, or third-party) in compliance with applicable data protection laws, including GDPR, CCPA, and other relevant regulations.

7.2.2. Personal data must only be collected, processed, and stored for legitimate business purposes and with appropriate safeguards.

7.2.3. Data breaches or suspected breaches must be reported to the IT Security team and the Data Protection Officer within **24 hours** of discovery.

### 7.3 Information Security

7.3.1. Employees must follow the Company's Information Security Policy, including:
- Using strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication
- Locking devices when unattended
- Encrypting sensitive data in transit and at rest
- Not sharing login credentials
- Reporting phishing attempts and suspicious activity to IT Security

7.3.2. Remote employees must adhere to additional security requirements as outlined in POL-003 Remote Work Policy, Section 5.

## 8. Reporting Violations

### 8.1 Reporting Channels

8.1.1. Employees may report suspected violations of this Code through the following channels:

| Channel | Contact | Anonymity |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Direct Manager | In person or email | Not anonymous |
| Human Resources | hr@meridiantech.com | Confidential |
| Ethics & Compliance | ethics@meridiantech.com | Confidential |
| Ethics Hotline | 1-800-555-0188 | **Anonymous available** |
| Online Portal | https://ethics.meridiantech.com | **Anonymous available** |

8.1.2. The Ethics Hotline and online portal are operated by an independent third party and are available **24 hours a day, 7 days a week**.

8.1.3. Employees may also report to any member of the senior leadership team.

### 8.2 Good Faith Reporting

8.2.1. All reports should be made in good faith, meaning the reporter reasonably believes the information is accurate.

8.2.2. Reports made in bad faith or with the intent to harm another person may result in disciplinary action against the reporter.

## 9. Investigation Process

### 9.1 Initial Assessment

9.1.1. All reports are acknowledged within **2 business days** of receipt.

9.1.2. The Ethics & Compliance team conducts a preliminary assessment to determine whether a formal investigation is warranted.

9.1.3. If the allegation involves a senior leader, the investigation will be overseen by the General Counsel or an external investigator.

### 9.2 Formal Investigation

9.2.1. Investigations are conducted promptly, thoroughly, and impartially.

9.2.2. The investigation may include:
- Interviews with the complainant, the respondent, and witnesses
- Review of documents, emails, messages, and other records
- Analysis of system logs and access records
- Consultation with legal counsel

9.2.3. All participants in an investigation are expected to cooperate fully and honestly. Refusal to cooperate may result in disciplinary action.

9.2.4. Investigations are treated as confidential to the extent possible. Information is shared only on a need-to-know basis.

### 9.3 Resolution

9.3.1. Upon conclusion of the investigation, the Ethics & Compliance team will document findings and recommendations.

9.3.2. The complainant will be informed that the investigation has been completed and whether corrective action has been taken (specific disciplinary details are not disclosed).

9.3.3. Investigation files are retained for a minimum of **7 years**.

## 10. Disciplinary Actions

### 10.1 Progressive Discipline

10.1.1. The Company follows a progressive discipline framework for policy violations:

| Step | Action | Documentation |
|------|--------|---------------|
| 1 | **Verbal Warning** | Documented in HRIS by manager |
| 2 | **Written Warning** | Formal letter signed by employee and manager |
| 3 | **Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)** | Structured plan with measurable criteria (see POL-005, Section 8) |
| 4 | **Termination** | Executed after consultation with HR and Legal |

10.1.2. The Company reserves the right to skip steps in cases of severe misconduct, including but not limited to theft, fraud, violence, threats of violence, substance abuse on Company premises, or gross negligence.

### 10.2 Factors Considered

10.2.1. When determining appropriate disciplinary action, the following factors are considered:
- Severity of the violation
- Whether the behavior was intentional or negligent
- The employee's disciplinary history
- Impact on affected parties, the team, and the organization
- Whether the employee self-reported the violation

### 10.3 Right to Respond

10.3.1. Employees subject to disciplinary action have the right to provide a written response, which will be included in their personnel file.

10.3.2. For termination decisions, the employee may request a review by the HR Business Partner before the decision is finalized.

## 11. Social Media Guidelines

### 11.1 Personal Social Media

11.1.1. Employees are entitled to personal expression on social media; however, they must not:
- Disclose confidential Company information
- Speak on behalf of the Company without authorization
- Post content that harasses, discriminates against, or threatens colleagues
- Use Company logos, trademarks, or branding without approval

11.1.2. Employees should include a disclaimer (e.g., "Views are my own") when discussing work-related topics on personal social media accounts.

### 11.2 Official Company Social Media

11.2.1. Only authorized employees (Communications, Marketing, and designated spokespersons) may post on the Company's official social media channels.

11.2.2. All official posts must be approved by the Communications team before publication.

## 12. Gift and Entertainment Policy

### 12.1 Receiving Gifts

12.1.1. Employees may accept unsolicited gifts of nominal value (under **$100** per occurrence) from clients, vendors, or business partners.

12.1.2. Gifts exceeding **$100** in value must be reported to the Ethics & Compliance team within **5 business days** of receipt. The team will determine whether the gift may be retained, must be returned, or should be donated to charity.

12.1.3. Cash or cash equivalents (gift cards, prepaid cards) may **never** be accepted regardless of value.

### 12.2 Giving Gifts

12.2.1. Employees may give business-related gifts valued at up to **$100** per recipient per occasion, provided the gift is reasonable, lawful, and does not create an appearance of impropriety.

12.2.2. Gifts to government officials are subject to strict legal restrictions and must be pre-approved by the Legal department.

### 12.3 Entertainment

12.3.1. Reasonable business meals and entertainment are permitted when they serve a legitimate business purpose and both the employee and the external party are present.

12.3.2. Lavish or extravagant entertainment must be pre-approved by the department head and reported to the Ethics & Compliance team.

### 12.4 Recordkeeping

12.4.1. All gifts given or received (regardless of value) must be logged in the Company's Gift & Entertainment Register, accessible on the intranet.

## 13. Whistleblower Protection

### 13.1 Non-Retaliation

13.1.1. The Company strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee who, in good faith:
- Reports a suspected violation of this Code, Company policy, or applicable law
- Participates in an investigation
- Refuses to engage in conduct that would violate this Code

13.1.2. Retaliation includes adverse employment actions such as termination, demotion, suspension, harassment, threats, or any other action that would discourage a reasonable person from reporting.

### 13.2 Reporting Retaliation

13.2.1. Employees who believe they have experienced retaliation should report it immediately through any of the channels listed in Section 8.1.

13.2.2. Retaliation claims are investigated with the same rigor and confidentiality as the underlying report.

### 13.3 Consequences of Retaliation

13.3.1. Any employee found to have engaged in retaliation will be subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination, regardless of whether the original report was substantiated.

### 13.4 External Reporting

13.4.1. Nothing in this Code prevents employees from reporting potential violations of law to the appropriate government agency (e.g., SEC, OSHA, EEOC) or from participating in government investigations. Employees are not required to notify the Company before making such reports.

## 14. Compliance Certification

14.1. All employees must acknowledge and certify their understanding of this Code annually through the Company's compliance certification process.

14.2. New employees certify their acknowledgment during onboarding (see POL-004 Onboarding Guide, Section 5.2).

14.3. Failure to complete the annual certification may result in restricted system access and will be noted in the employee's performance record.

## 15. Policy Administration

15.1. The Ethics & Compliance team, in partnership with Human Resources and the Legal department, is responsible for the administration, interpretation, and enforcement of this Code.

15.2. This Code is reviewed annually and updated as needed to reflect changes in law, regulation, or business practice.

15.3. Questions or clarifications should be directed to ethics@meridiantech.com.
